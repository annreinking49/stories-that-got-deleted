Title: The End of the World
Author: Giantboyslit
Source: https://archiveofourown.org/works/91817246
Tags: Macro/Micro, Giants, Vore, Apocalypse, Shotacon

Summary:
The world is ending. Once a week, millions are spirited away by a god. Thankfully, Mark has his little brother and a friend he made online to help him retain hope...

==================================================

Mark meandered through the grey streets. He enjoyed the absolute silence that preceded each Taking. Just needed to avoid thinking about what came after the silence.

Mark missed the trees. There were almost none left these days. Nobody wants to plant trees whose shadow nobody will lounge under. The existing trees died when everyone stopped watering them. The government had other priorities since it all started. And with the near-constant datkness, few plants survived, even in nature.

"Be a hero, volunteer!" said a flyer poorly taped on the base of a broken streetlamp. The drawing on it showed a hand larger than anything, larger than a city, plucking up an entire crowd. The real hand that did the Taking was way bigger and scarier, Mark mused.

Just three years ago, society had been normal. Mark was a fresh-faced middle school student whose worries were girls and getting good grades. Then the sky had gone dark one day, and the hand had come once a week since, taking a few millions each time. Mark's dad had been taken with the first batch, before he could see the entire world collapse; before he could have seen the life leave his son's eyes.

But Mark had been among the lucky ones. As he wandered near the tent city where thousands huddled in fear and utter silence, he wordlessly thanked whatever god decided to forsake humanity only after Mark had had a taste of happiness, a crumb of hope.

That was why he was afraid to go home. Ian, his little brother, was merely 7. Ian had never known a world that believed it had a future. 

Ian had never gone to school, never learned to read... What use were books when all of humanity would vanish within a few years?

His phone buzzed. Mark opened it and smiled to himself. Benji was the only person Mark knew who hadn't lost hope. Sure, there were the fanatics convinced that the Taking led to Heaven, to a world of bliss closer to God. But Benji's hope was different. Pure. In fact, it was like Benji had no clue what hopelessness was. 

Despite being just older than Ian, Benji was literate and seemingly went to school like a normal boy from the before world. That was why Mark so desperately insisted to chat with the younger boy, day after day since they met by accident on Reddit, even when Benji's average problem was complaining that his teacher gave a smiling sticker to another girl in his class and not him, even though Benji read a full paragraph out loud at the blackboard.

"Check it out!" the text said, together with a photo of a poorly drawn house with two stick figures nearby. "Did it for art class! Miss Spinks says I did good."

"Beautiful drawing, I'll show Ian," Mark replied. A truck screeched to a stop near him, and government agents poured out before manhandling some of the homeless people, forcing them in the back. Mark rapidly turned heel and headed home. "The preparations for the Taking started here. Is your area safe?"

Mark was halfway home when Benji replied. "\[Laughing emoji\] yeah dw lol" 

Mark wished Benji would take the Taking more seriously. From west coast to east coast, from Paris to Beijing, millions were rounded up every week, but Benji seemed to find the idea plainly funny. Mark assumed his younger friend was the son of someone important, someone who wouldn't be picked up at random. He'd never met Benji or even got a real answer as to where he lived. Mark assumed somewhere with great outdoor lighting, given that Benji's photos sometimes included what looked uncannily like sunlight—an impossibility since the sun vanished.

Mark still texted him "Take care" before getting home.

The silence had ended, alas, turning into screams and human waves, crowds pushing and shoving their neighbors in a desperate hope not to be Taken, to survive one week longer. Mark sulked in the shadiws until he got through the front door of his apartment building.

Once upon a time, some fiction writers imagined that the apocalypse would be epic, bombastic... but it was just depressing. God had just decided to torture humanity into extinction one day, and society collapsed, leaving only pain and fear.

Mark's blood ran cold when he reached his floor. His apartment's door had been caved in.

"No... Nononono NO!!" Mark screamed, sudden panic activating all of his nerves at once. He leaped inside the unit. "Ian... Fuck, please, Ian, no..." He couldn't stop the tears.

Nothing had been stolen. But Ian was gone. Rounded up for the Taking.

Mark was halfway down the street, running at full tilt towards the holding camp where the victims of the next Taking were held, when his phone rang again.

"Soooo... seen any good movies lately? :)"

"not now benji" he texted back without slowing down. WHO thought about movies during a Taking?! Benji's usually adorable innocence felt grating, for once.

"Aww come on :3" Benji replied almost instantly. "I saw a cool anime movie about fighting mouses!"

Mark ignored it, but the phone rang again. "MICE! Haha Mrs Forcible says I'm really good with granmar!"

Mark needed to take a break to catch up his breath anyway; he used that brief respite to explain: "My little brother was grabbed for the Taking. Am gonna save him. No time to chat."

Benji's response made Mark experience anger like never before, followed by bitter horror: "Ian was taken? SO COOL! \[dancing emoji\]" ... "Can't wait to meet him :D I'm wearing a red Dr Willy tshirt!"

Benji, despite his cheerfulness... had been Taken too? The idea that Ian and Benji were both about to disappear from the world forever broke something deep within Mark's soul. He knew that the cultists were all morons. Being Taken meant dying, no matter what the optimistic fools believed.

"I'm wearing a black hoodie" Mark texted Benji after a brief reflection. He then snapped and sent a selfie as he was.

Benji and he would finally meet.

When he made it to the holding camp, Mark didn't ask for Ian to be freed; he knew it would be useless. That day, three years ago, god had given humanity a single order: "Every week. That same amount." His hand had been filled with a compact mass of people at the time. The only time the government hadn't delivered the required sacrifice, god had wiped out most of Italy. Sacrifices for the Taking were never released, since then.

No, Mark didn't ask for Ian's freedom. Instead, he offered himself as volunteer, and he was tossed among the sheer ocean of terrified humans like one more rice onto an already bursting bowl, awaiting the chopsticks.

"Ian? IAN!!" Mark yelled, desperately shoving his way across the crowd. He couldn't see his little brother...

"Rejoice! We are God's chosen!" squeaked an old lady in a shawl who blocked Mark's path.

"Sorry... Excuse me, I'm looking for my brother," Mark said, trying to push past the lunatic.

"No, no, you're worrying about nothing," she insisted, grabbing his arm with her knotted, spindly fingers. "Your brother will soon be safe in God's own bosom!"

At the same moment, Mark's phone buzzed. "Are you in place? I can't wait ^w^" Benji texted.

"Im in the holding center looking for ian," Mark replied.

"COOL! Starting rn" was Benji's near-immediate response. It was followed by a gif from some cartoon Mark had never seen, depicting an anime boy licking his lips while patting his belly. What?

Before he could snap out of his confusion, the eternal darkness surrounding the Earth suddenly split into blinding light.

"It's starting!" the faithful crone crowed uselessly in Mark's ear. It was the same every week. Light flooded the world, so intense that it fried every recording instrument and could hardly be tolerated by any humanlooking at it. Then, a hand half the size of a city emerged from the light, and...

Mark ovserved as well he could between his barely-spread fingers as the sky was replaced by flesh rapidly descending towards Earth. In an instant, the gigantic fingers raked the ground, razing entire neighborhoods, to close a first around the sacrifices. For Mark, it meant suddenly seeing and feeling every body in a 100 foot radius being compressed into him, and after a minute of suffocating in oven-like body heat, being released at the top of a human pile. Most people under him were still alive, but the faithful old woman laid dead, her neck snapped by the rollercoaster.

"IAN! Are you alive, Ian?!" Mark screamed, resuming his search for his brother.

Even though his eyes swept left and right multiple times, his brain needed a minute to catch up. Perhaps it was because the red seemed like the color of the sky in the horizon, maybe because the logo was so far it was blurred, its colors dampened by a massive chunk of atmosphere... but the horizon looked intensely like the face of video game character Dr Willy...

"No... No fucking way..." Mark whispered.

Under Mark's feet, thousands of innocent peopke, tossed into a careless pile, writhed and struggled to survive. And above, far above Mark's head, throning above a red t-shirt stamp with Dr Willy's cartoon face, was Benji's ever-smiling face. Benji's messy blond hair framed his round and innocent face, and, just like in the photos Mark had seen, Benji was missing a tooth in his big wide boyish grin... except that a hundred people could stand shoulder to shoulder in that tooth gap easily.

"Uh, I guess I shoulda asked you to wear colors that are easy to see haha... Can't see ya at all!" Benji said, seemingly to the five million-ish people displaced by the Taking. "Hey, so uh, can ya like... jump in place or something, Mark?" Benji asked, not losing the grin.

Mark was frozen, but not everyone was. A few men started jumping up and down, making wide arm movements—Mark briefly wondered if they were all named Mark and thought the message was for them. But then, women started jumping. People of all genders, races and ages jumped desperately, hoping to get this 9 year old god's favor.

Benji had raised one hand, seemingly about to pluck the first jumper off the pile, but he froze and grimaced when more joined in. "Hey guys, not cool. You're not my friend Mark."

Mark joined in in the jumping and hollering. Benji seemed to still consider him special... being lost in the crowd would be the most useless fate imaginable. Far in the sky, Benji reached far out of view—the horizon, now slowly stabilizing into view, seemed to be an elementary school boy's bedroom, and Benji was grabbing something on a desk the size of the Earth—and the boy god brandished a magnifying glass.

"Don't make this hard... you guys are smaller than ants, y'know?" Benji whined. It was definitely a whine, as if the last desperate flailing of five million humans' survival instincts were just kinda annoying to him.

The process took several minutes, but to Mark's relief, Benji immediately recognized him when his magnifying glass stopped in the sky over him. 

"Hop on, Mark!" Benji said, presenting the tip of his index. "Hey! Just Mark!" he complained when a few dozen people desperately jumped on the offered finger.

The colossal little boy checked that Mark was still down ontop the pile before lifting his finger high, hiiiigh in the air and, without hesitation, shoving it into his mouth. Mark stared up, dumbfounded, as Benji wrapped his lips around the finger and sloppily yanked it out. Not a single person remained on the fingertip. Benji casually swallowed, then offered the finger to Mark again. It was covered in a film of drool—a single drop from it effortlessly engulfed several people lower on the pile, who struggled, unable to break the surface tension of one drop. Regardless, Mark daren't say no. He started to climb toward the boy's fingernail, only to find himself glued, hands and feet alike, to Benji's rapidly drying saliva. Regardless, the giant boy seemed satisfied and brought him up to eye level, where the boy's cheerful face took up the entirety of Mark's field of view and then some.

  
"It's so cool meetincha in person!" Benji bellowed. Mark wanted to slam his hands onto his ears—from up close, the volume was closer to a nuclear explosion than a human voice. 

"Ian... please find Ian..." Mark begged the planetary-sized child.

"It's always so fun chattin' with ya! But uh, guess I can't hear whatcha say hahaha!" 

As Benji laughed, Mark felt a piercing pain in his ears and a warm liquid pooling inside. His eardrums had quite literally exploded, leaving him dazed. Well, more dazed.

"You must... save Ian..." Mark supplicated. Glued as he was, a bug to a bug compared to Benji he even seemed like he was prostrated already.

Not hearing him, and with the casual confidence of a well-practiced gesture, Benji scooped a few hundred thousand people in my cupper fingers and brought them to his face. He opened his mouth, a dark abyss that could swallow a country, and simply tossed the people inside, deaf to Mark's howls of despair.

"Y'know, when dad bought me some lame-ass planet from the Milky Way, I was so bummed," Benji casually talked towards the speck on his raised fingertip.

The colossus scooped more. Mark fought like a devil to unglue himself, in vain. He cried and screamed and prayed. Benji's movements didn't react one bit. "I'm glad it was your lame-ass planet though, you're a cool big bro!" Benji tossed another half million people onto his eldritch-sized tongue. They seemed to dissolve on contact, but they truly disappeared between taste buds the size of the Appalachian or swallowed up by literal oceans of drool. "Never woulda metya without that, so I gave daddy the noodle collar I made at school. To say thanks. Cause you're my friend..." Benji blushed and averted his gaze, looking bashfully down before his signature grin returned, beaming at Mark.

Mark stared, almost catatonic, wondering if Ian was dead yet.

Benji shyly blushed and twisted one strand of hair around his other hàd's finger. "So, uh, wouldya like to live here? I-I mean, I could get a little bug terrarium, feed you seeds or something..." Benji seemed to wait for something, then: "Well? Uh, you can text me, you know? I mean, your answer..."

His phone! Mark snapped out of his stupor. He yanked uselessly at his hands, but they remained firmly glued to the "ground" that was the fingertip the size of Paris. In the distance, Benji was seemingly snacking out of nervosity... by scooping countless other people. Crying, begging people with families and lives. 

Mark tore his right hand free in a sickening ripping, bloody mess. A lot of his skin remained attached to Benji, but as long as he was careful not to get blood on his phone, he could text Benji. Fast! Mark's heart nearly stopped when he saw the ominous "2%" next to the battery symbol.

"stop eat ppl! no more taking," read his first text. 

Benji... finished swallowing his mouthful before glancing at his computer screen. Benji's face beamed when he saw the text, then frowned a little bit.

"Aw c'mon, don't be a killjoy," Benji pouted. "I like the taste."

Mark's butchered fingers were flying across the screen to reply. "important!!! no more taking" and "why taking??" as well as "we are people" and "ian! FIND IAN!!!"

Benji leaned back on his desk chair and turtled his head into his shoulders somewhat, like a disappointed kid hoping to make himself cute enough to reverse whatever punishment was enacted on him. "Fiiiiiiiiine," Benji sighed out.

"Ian alive??" was Mark's last text before his phone winked off. Benji wasn't really looking at his instant messenger anymore, however.

"I, uh, I mean it's a dumb story, you're gonna laugh," Benji told the barely perceptible speck on his fingertip with a little awkward laugh. "I was 6 when I first grabbed those people, y'know? And like, it tasted good! I don't wanna be like, I mean y'know like—remember that asshole Benny at school? Well, his dad bought him new sneakers you know? And-"

Mark wasn't even listening to the little boy's blabbering anymore. He just swayed weakly. Shattered. His father had been a snack. Ian would be a snack. Even as he prattled on, without even noticing he was doing it, Benji was wiping his fingertip across the terrified crowd, plucking a few hundreds at a time, and sucking them off his finger subconsciously. Mark wasn't even strong enough to hope, anymore. Maybe it was the blood loss from his still torn-open arm. Maybe the existential shock.

"Anyway! What do you guys eat? You can eat the same things that I can right? You should try a bite of my fave snack!" Benji exclaimed to Mark. 

The world shifted for a minute. Mark's perspective was far too minuscule to understand the details of it, but Benji had moved him, scraped him off his fingertip and tearing another chunk if flesh off Mark. He was now in some sort of... glass landscape, like the Roman Coliseum.

A petri dish. He'd been dropped in a petri dish.

Impossibly far above, Benji's face addressed him a big boyish grin. "Enjoy the meal!" Then, absurdly proportioned fingers blotted out the sun—Benji's face—and sprinkled something in the petri dish around Mark.

It was bodies. It rained bodies. Leftovers from Benji's little snack that stuck to the pores of his skin, trapped in the infinitesimal grooves and imperfections of a boy the size of a planet.

Mark cried in despair and pain when he recognized Ian's small lifeless body in the mass.

"My favorite meal is humans from Earth! Enjoy it!" Benji intoned happily, certain he'd done a good action. "If you like it, don't worry! I get more every week!"
